Benchmade 940 Osborne Buyer's Guide & Expert Review

If you carry a pocket knife every single day, you eventually realize that the biggest friction point isn't cutting performance—it’s pocket footprint. Many heavy-duty folders feel like a brick in your trousers, while lightweight models often flex under lateral loads. Enter Warren Osborne’s legendary design: the Benchmade 940 Osborne.

For over two decades, this folder has remained the gold standard for daily utility. It bridges the gap between a robust tactical folder and a sleek gentleman's carry. Priced at approximately CAD $280.81, it is a significant investment. This in-depth guide breaks down the engineering, handles real-world usage, compares it to top rivals, and helps you decide if it deserves a spot in your pocket.

Engineering & Real-World Usability Breakdown

To understand why the Benchmade 940 commands its price point, you have to look past the marketing and examine the metallurgy, mechanical tolerances, and geometry.

1. Blade Steel: CPM-S30V Performance

The 940 features a 3.40-inch blade crafted from CPM-S30V premium stainless steel, heat-treated to a rockwell hardness of 58-60 HRC. Developed specifically for high-end cutlery by Crucible Industries, S30V relies on vanadium carbides to achieve an exceptional balance between edge retention, corrosion resistance, and toughness.

In day-to-day cutting tasks—ranging from breaking down thick corrugated cardboard to stripping electrical wire and cutting zip ties—the factory edge holds its working sharpness for weeks of heavy professional use. When it finally does come time to sharpen, S30V takes a clean, razor-sharp edge on diamond stones without requiring specialized ceramic equipment, though you will need to spend a little more time on the stones compared to softer steels like 14C28N.

2. Geometry: The Reverse Tanto Advantage

Unlike a traditional drop point or clip point, the 940 utilizes a reverse tanto profile. The spine of the blade is straight and flat until it abruptly angles down to the tip. This leaves a thick, robust spine running almost all the way to the point, giving the knife extraordinary tip strength. If you regularly pry open paint cans, punch holes in heavy plastics, or dig out splintered wood, the tip of the 940 will not chip or roll as easily as a finer drop point. Despite the aggressive tip, the primary flat grind tapers down nicely, ensuring the knife still excels at slicing fruit, food prep, and fine utility cuts.

3. Handle Architecture & Ergonomics

The handles are constructed from 6061-T6 aircraft-grade aluminum finished in a distinct anodized olive green, paired with purple titanium spacers that give the knife its classic look. Embedded stainless steel liners provide rigid structural integrity, ensuring the handle doesn't flex when you squeeze down hard during heavy push-cuts.

At a slim 0.41 inches thick, the handle disappears into the seam of your jeans or the corner of your pocket. However, this slimness is a double-edged sword. Users with extra-large hands or those wearing thick work gloves may find the handle fills the hand less securely than a contoured G10 handle. The anodized aluminum also feels slicker than textured composite scales, though Benchmade added subtle line jimping along the spine and finger choil to maintain purchase.

4. The AXIS Lock Mechanism

Benchmade's patented AXIS locking mechanism is the crown jewel of the 940's operation. Driven by a small omega spring-tensioned steel bar that slides forward to lock securely into the blade tang, it completely eliminates the risk of accidental finger closure during hard downward cuts.

Because the lock is fully ambidextrous, left-handed and right-handed users can flick the knife open or closed with zero wrist strain. Simply pull back on the dual thumb studs, disengage the AXIS bar, and the blade swings freely on its phosphor bronze washers.

Benchmade 940 vs. Spyderco Paramilitary 2 (PM2)

The PM2 is the 940's most frequent rival. The PM2 offers superior ergonomic contouring, a wider blade with exceptional slicing geometry, and Spyderco's ultra-reliable Compression Lock. However, the PM2 has a notoriously wide, leaf-shaped profile in the pocket—it takes up significantly more real estate than the 940. If you wear slim-fit office attire or fitted jeans, the 940 slides in and out effortlessly, whereas the PM2 feels like carrying a broader tool.

Benchmade 940 vs. Zero Tolerance 0450

Designed by Dmitry Sinkevich, the ZT 0450 is an ultra-premium titanium frame-lock folder with a KVT ball-bearing pivot. It is lighter and boasts a high-end aesthetic. That said, the titanium scales on the ZT 0450 can feel slippery in wet conditions, and its pocket clip creates a pronounced hotspot during extended carving sessions. The 940's aluminum handle and dual thumb studs offer more utilitarian comfort for actual work environments.

Balanced Pros & Cons

No pocket knife is universally perfect for every user. Here is an honest appraisal of where the Benchmade 940 shines and where it falls short.

Pros

  • Class-Leading Weight-to-Strength Ratio: Weighing just 2.9 ounces, it carries invisibly in light shorts or heavy work trousers.
  • Durable Point Geometry: The reverse tanto tip resists snapping and bending under severe torsional stress.
  • True Ambidextrous Design: Reversible pocket clip combined with the AXIS lock makes it equally friendly for left- and right-handed users.
  • Timeless Aesthetic: The green and purple colorway is an iconic design piece recognized instantly by knife enthusiasts worldwide.
  • Resale & Support: Backed by Benchmade’s lifetime warranty and LifeSharp service, which sharpens and tunes your knife for free (minus shipping).

Cons

  • Factory Stiffness: Out-of-the-box action can feel tight; pivot screws often require micro-adjustments to achieve drop-shut freedom.
  • Aluminum Scratches: The green anodized finish can show wear, scuffs, and dings if dropped against concrete or carried in a pocket alongside keys.
  • Ergonomics for Large Hands: The slim 0.41-inch handle thickness lacks the palm-filling fullness of thicker tactical folders.
  • Price Point: Premium materials command a steep entry fee that casual users may find hard to justify.

Search-Optimized FAQ

Is the Benchmade 940 legal to carry everywhere?

Legality depends entirely on your local, regional, and national knife laws. The 940 features a 3.4-inch blade, which complies with jurisdictions allowing blades under 3.5 inches. However, automatic opening laws do not apply here because the 940 is a manual opening folder, deployed via thumb studs rather than a spring-assisted mechanism. Always check municipal bylaws regarding blade length and locking mechanisms before traveling.

How do I maintain and sharpen the S30V steel blade?

CPM-S30V holds its edge exceptionally well, meaning you won't need to sharpen it frequently. When it does lose its working bite, diamond hones or ceramic stones work best due to the high vanadium carbide content. For routine maintenance, keep the pivot clean of pocket lint with compressed air and apply a drop of mineral oil or lightweight synthetic lubricant to the washers every few months.

What is Benchmade's LifeSharp service?

Benchmade offers a complimentary factory service called LifeSharp. If you send your 940 to their headquarters in Oregon, their technicians will clean, re-sharpen, oil, and tune your knife to factory specifications for free—you only pay postage to ship it to them. They will also replace broken omega springs or damaged screws for a nominal fee or under warranty.
