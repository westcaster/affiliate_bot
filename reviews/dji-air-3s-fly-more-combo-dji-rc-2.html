DJI Air 3S Fly More Combo (DJI RC 2) Buyer's Guide & Expert Review

For professional creators and advanced hobbyists searching for a mid-tier drone that refuses to compromise on sensor size or flight safety, the landscape has fundamentally shifted. The release of the DJI Air 3S bridges the long-standing gap between compact travel drones and high-end cinematography platforms.

Priced at $1,399.00 for the Fly More Combo with the DJI RC 2, this kit bundles the aircraft with three high-capacity batteries, a multi-battery charging hub, a convertible shoulder bag, and an exceptional screen-integrated controller. Over the past three weeks, I put this platform through coastal winds, dim architectural twilight shoots, and complex urban obstacle environments. This comprehensive review analyzes how the Air 3S performs outside the marketing lab.

Quick Verdict: 4.8 / 5.0

The DJI Air 3S Fly More Combo is the most capable mid-weight aerial cinematography tool currently available. By pairing a massive 1-inch CMOS primary sensor with a dedicated 70mm telephoto lens, DJI has solved the primary compromise of dual-camera drones: uneven image quality between focal lengths.

Key Takeaways:

  • Stunning Low-Light Capabilities: The 1-inch primary sensor paired with 14 stops of dynamic range delivers remarkably clean night and twilight footage.
  • True Dual-Camera Parity: Unlike older iterations, the 70mm medium telephoto lens now utilizes the same advanced image processing algorithms, minimizing color shift during lens switches.
  • Unmatched Safety Architecture: Omnidirectional obstacle sensing enhanced by forward-facing LiDAR makes nighttime and complex-terrain return-to-home maneuvers remarkably stress-free.
  • Exceptional Value Bundle: The Fly More package effectively eliminates battery anxiety, providing nearly 2.5 hours of cumulative flight time out of the box.

In-Depth Engineering & Real-World Usability

Chassis Construction and Material Tolerances

Taking the Air 3S out of its carrying case, the immediate impression is one of dense, purposeful engineering. Weighing in at 724 grams, the folding frame utilizes high-impact composite plastics reinforced at major stress joints, particularly around the motor mounts and hinge mechanisms. The joints exhibit zero lateral play, resisting the torsional flexing that cheaper platforms display in high-wind conditions.

Dual-Camera Architecture

The headline feature is the dual-primary camera gimbal assembly.
  • The Main Camera: Features a 50MP 1-inch CMOS sensor with a 24mm equivalent focal length and an f/1.8 aperture. In the field, the larger photosites capture vastly superior shadow detail compared to the 1/1.3-inch sensors of its predecessor. Shooting in D-Log M at 4K/60fps provides ample grading latitude, pulling rich color information out of high-contrast sunset scenes without introducing muddy shadow noise.
  • The Telephoto Camera: Features a 48MP 1/1.3-inch sensor with a 70mm (3x optical equivalent) focal length and an f/2.8 aperture. While its sensor is physically smaller than the main camera, its pixel-binning performance matches color science so closely that cutting between 24mm and 70mm in post-production requires minimal color matching. It is an exceptional tool for flattening perspective during landscape compression or isolating subjects safely from a distance.

Omnidirectional Sensing and LiDAR Integration

Where the Air 3S truly earns its keep for professional operators is the upgraded sensor suite. DJI has integrated a forward-facing LiDAR sensor alongside standard vision positioning cameras. During a dusk test flight through a densely wooded park, the aircraft actively painted branches and ground contours that traditionally fool standard optical-only systems. Combined with the upgraded Smart RTH (Return-to-Home) feature—which plots a safe flight path even when flying through unlit environments—the drone provides a tangible psychological safety net.

O4 Video Transmission and Ergonomics

The included DJI RC 2 controller remains a gold standard for field ergonomics. Weighing 420 grams, its textured grip panels prevent slippage during damp coastal shoots. The built-in 5.5-inch FHD screen achieves 700 nits of peak brightness, remaining fully legible under glaring midday sun without requiring a shade hood. Powered by the O4 transmission protocol, the video feed remained rock-solid, maintaining a crisp 1080p/60fps preview at a distance of 3.2 miles over rolling hills, completely free of stutter or frame dropping.

Versus the DJI Air 3

While both drones share the same 45-minute battery life and O4 transmission backbone, the Air 3S justifies its price premium through the massive upgrade to a 1-inch primary sensor. If you frequently shoot during golden hour, blue hour, or night environments, the reduction in noise and boost in dynamic range on the Air 3S is immediately apparent. The addition of forward LiDAR also makes the Air 3S significantly safer for automated nocturnal waypoint missions.

Versus the DJI Mini 4 Pro

The Mini 4 Pro wins on pure portability (under 249 grams), making it attractive for travelers seeking regulatory leniency. However, it lacks a dedicated optical telephoto lens and struggles significantly more in high winds due to its lighter mass. The Air 3S provides professional wind resistance, a larger sensor, and true focal length versatility that structural and commercial real estate shooters require.

Balanced Pros & Cons

Pros:

  • Superb Low-Light Performance: The 1-inch sensor captures remarkably clean footage in dim environments where smaller sensors introduce heavy chroma noise.
  • LiDAR-Assisted Safety: Advanced night-sensing capabilities make automated return-to-home and obstacle avoidance much more reliable after sunset.
  • Comprehensive Fly More Bundle: Includes three batteries, an intelligent charging hub that power-banks excess energy, and an ND filter set readiness.
  • Seamless Dual-Lens Workflow: Consistent color science between the 24mm and 70mm cameras speeds up editing timelines.

Cons:

  • Higher Price Point: At $1,399.00, it approaches the entry threshold of professional Micro Four Thirds platforms.
  • Regulatory Classification: At 724 grams, it falls squarely into stricter category tiers under international aviation authorities (such as FAA Part 107 requirements in the US).
  • Fixed Aperture on Telephoto: The 70mm telephoto lens maintains a fixed f/2.8 aperture, requiring physical ND filters for shutter speed control in bright daylight.

Target Audience Profile

Who Should Buy This Drone:

  • Real Estate & Architectural Photographers: The 70mm telephoto lens captures compressed, distortion-free exterior angles, while the 1-inch primary sensor handles dim interior rooms with ease.
  • Travel & Documentary Filmmakers: Creators who need a lightweight, packable kit that can punch above its weight class in variable lighting conditions without sacrificing cinematic color profiles (10-bit D-Log M).
  • Advanced Hobbyists: Enthusiasts upgrading from older Mini or Air series drones who want professional-grade safety sensors and extended battery endurance.

Who Should Skip This Drone:

  • Casual Vacationers: If you only fly twice a year on vacation, the DJI Mini 4 Pro offers adequate performance at a lower financial commitment.
  • Hollywood Feature Film Crews: Productions requiring interchangeable cinema lenses and Super35 or Full-Frame sensors should look toward heavy-lift enterprise platforms like the DJI Inspire 3.

Search-Optimized FAQ

Does the DJI Air 3S Fly More Combo require an FAA license in the United States?

Yes. With a takeoff weight of 724 grams, the DJI Air 3S exceeds the 250-gram threshold established by the Federal Aviation Administration. If you are flying for recreational purposes, you must pass the TRUST exam and register the drone. If you are flying for any commercial purpose (real estate, YouTube monetization, inspection), you must operate under FAA Part 107 guidelines.

Can the DJI RC 2 screen be viewed easily in direct sunlight?

Yes. The built-in 5.5-inch FHD screen on the DJI RC 2 produces up to 700 nits of luminance. During real-world testing under glaring afternoon desert sun, interface icons and the live video feed remained clearly visible without requiring an aftermarket sunshade.

What is the advantage of the LiDAR sensor on the Air 3S?

Traditional obstacle avoidance systems rely exclusively on optical cameras, which struggle to detect obstacles in low-light or unlit environments. The addition of forward-facing LiDAR on the Air 3S allows the aircraft to actively measure distance using laser pulses, providing reliable obstacle sensing and safe Return-to-Home routing even during twilight and night operations.
