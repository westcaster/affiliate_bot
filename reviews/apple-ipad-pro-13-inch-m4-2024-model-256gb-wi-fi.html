Apple iPad Pro 13-Inch (M4, 2024 Model, 256GB, Wi-Fi): The Definitive Buyer’s Guide & Review

For years, the perennial criticism leveled against Apple’s flagship tablets was not a lack of processing capability, but a software ecosystem and hardware form factor caught between traditional computing and mobile convenience. The introduction of the Apple iPad Pro 13-Inch (M4, 2024 Model) fundamentally shifts that paradigm. Dropping down to an astonishing 5.1mm thickness while migrating to a tandem OLED architecture, this tablet aims to bridge the gap between studio-grade production machinery and ultra-portable media consumption.

At an estimated starting price of $1,299.00 for the 256GB Wi-Fi configuration, this machine commands a heavy premium. This comprehensive, hands-on review examines whether the engineering marvel packed into this ultra-thin chassis justifies the investment for creative professionals, power users, and everyday enthusiasts.

Quick Verdict

Overall Score: 4.8 / 5.0

The 2024 13-inch iPad Pro with the M4 chip represents a watershed moment in mobile computing hardware. By solving historical OLED brightness constraints through tandem technology and housing it in an impossibly thin aluminum enclosure, Apple has created a formidable creative tool.

  • Design & Portability: 5.1mm thickness makes a 13-inch footprint shockingly easy to manage.
  • Display Quality: Ultra Retina XDR tandem OLED delivers true blacks and 1,000 nits of full-screen brightness.
  • Performance: M4 chip handles 4K video rendering and 3D ray tracing with thermal headroom to spare.
  • Value Consideration: The base 256GB tier requires external storage management for heavy pro workflows, and iPadOS remains a point of debate for power users.

In-Engineering & Real-World Usability Breakdown

1. The 5.1mm Chassis and Aerospace-Grade Aluminum

Holding the 2024 13-inch iPad Pro for the first time induces a momentary cognitive dissonance. Measuring just 5.1mm thick and weighing 1.28 pounds, it is thinner than the fourth-generation iPod Nano. Apple achieved this by restructuring internal battery layouts and utilizing a 100% recycled aerospace-grade aluminum unibody that distributes structural rigidity evenly.

In real-world handheld use—whether reading architectural blueprints, sketching digital concepts on a couch, or reviewing raw footage on set—the reduced weight drastically cuts down wrist fatigue compared to the bulky 2022 M2 iteration. However, this thinness introduces a psychological caution: it feels delicate, making a secure case or folio practically mandatory for field use.

2. Ultra Retina XDR Display: Tandem OLED Technology

The display is the star of the show. Apple utilizes a "tandem OLED" setup—stacking two OLED panels on top of each other—to overcome the traditional brightness limitations of organic light-emitting diodes.
  • SDR & HDR Performance: It delivers 1,000 nits of full-screen brightness for standard and HDR content, peaking at 1,600 nits for HDR highlights.
  • Contrast & True Blacks: Because each pixel emits its own light and can turn completely off, contrast ratio sits at an astronomical 2,000,000:1.
  • Color Accuracy: For colorists and photographers working in P3 wide color gamuts, shadow detail separation in dark scenes is exceptionally crisp. There is no visible blooming, a common artifact in legacy mini-LED panels.

3. Processing Might: The Apple M4 Chip

Equipped with the M4 chip featuring a 9-core CPU (3 performance cores and 6 efficiency cores) and a 10-core GPU, this tablet obliterates mobile benchmark tests and competes directly with desktop-class silicon.

  • Real-World Creative Workflows: Exporting a 4K timeline in DaVinci Resolve or managing multi-layer vector illustrations in Affinity Designer reveals zero stutter.
Hardware-Accelerated Ray Tracing: The GPU introduces hardware-accelerated ray tracing and mesh shading. In titles like Zenless Zone Zero or 3D rendering environments like Blender and Octane X*, lighting bounces, reflections, and shadow realism approach console-grade fidelity.
  • Thermal Dynamics: Despite the 5.1mm chassis, thermal management is surprisingly adept. During prolonged rendering sessions, the back aluminum plate warms up uniformly to dissipate heat, preventing sudden thermal throttling.

Balanced Pros & Cons

Pros

  • Unrivaled Display Technology: Tandem OLED provides unmatched black levels, crisp contrast, and high daytime legibility.
  • Exemplary Form Factor: In-hand feel is revolutionary for a 13-inch screen size.
  • Desktop-Grade CPU/GPU: M4 architecture handles intensive video rendering, audio multi-tracking, and 3D modeling without breaking a sweat.
  • Ecosystem Integration: Seamless pairing with MacBooks (Sidecar/Universal Control), Apple Pencils, and Magic Keyboards.

Cons

  • Steep Entry Investment: The $1,299 base price excludes essential peripherals like the Apple Pencil Pro ($129) and Magic Keyboard ($349).
  • Storage Constraints at Base Tier: 256GB fills up rapidly when caching 4K video footage, RAW photo libraries, and heavy application caches.
  • iPadOS Limitations: Multi-tasking window management and background file processing still feel restricted compared to macOS or Windows.

Target Audience Profile

Who Should Buy This Device?

  • Digital Illustrators & Animators: Professionals relying on Procreate, Fresco, and Adobe Illustrator who want near-zero latency, pressure-sensitive precision, and peerless color grading.
  • Mobile Videographers & Field Editors: Creators shooting ProRes footage on iPhones or mirrorless cameras who need to dump cards, edit in LumaFusion or Final Cut Pro for iPad, and export while traveling.

  • Developers & Programmers Requiring Terminal Access: Those who need local compiling environments, custom IDE setups, and unrestricted file system management will find iPadOS too sandboxed. A MacBook Pro or Windows laptop remains necessary.

Search-Optimized FAQ

Is the 256GB storage capacity enough for the M4 iPad Pro 13-inch?

For users whose workflows rely on cloud storage (iCloud, Google Drive, Dropbox) and streaming media, 256GB is sufficient. However, if you regularly shoot 4K ProRes video, cache offline music and movie libraries, or install massive creative suites (like large Procreate galleries and 3D assets), you will hit storage caps quickly. Pro users should carefully weigh upgrading to the 512GB or 1TB models, which also bump RAM from 8GB to 16GB.

Does the 2024 M4 iPad Pro work with older Apple Pencils and Magic Keyboards?

No. Due to the radically thinner 5.1mm chassis design and a redesigned magnetic alignment layout for the front-facing camera (now oriented on the long landscape edge), the 2024 iPad Pro requires the Apple Pencil Pro and the newly redesigned iPad Pro Magic Keyboard. Legacy Magic Keyboards and Apple Pencil 2nd Generation units are physically and electronically incompatible.

How noticeable is the shift from mini-LED (2022) to Tandem OLED (2024)?

The difference is striking, particularly in dark environments. Older mini-LED iPad Pros suffered from light blooming around bright white text on black backgrounds due to zone-dimming limitations. The tandem OLED panel eliminates blooming entirely because every individual pixel controls its own illumination, yielding deep, inking blacks alongside significantly punchier HDR highlights.

Final Buying Verdict

The Apple iPad Pro 13-Inch (M4, 2024 Model, 256GB, Wi-Fi) is an engineering triumph that successfully redefines what a large-format tablet can achieve. By wedding a stunning tandem OLED display to the raw horsepower of the M4 chip inside a razor-thin chassis, Apple has created an uncompromised mobile canvas for digital creators.

While the ecosystem limitations of iPadOS and the steep entry cost—compounded by necessary accessory purchases—mean it is not a universal laptop replacement for every user, its hardware superiority is unquestionable. For creative professionals, designers, and mobile power users willing to adapt their workflows to a touch-first environment, this tablet stands alone at the pinnacle of modern computing hardware.
